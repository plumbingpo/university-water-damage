Bathroom Water Damage: What University Homeowners Should Know

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. We help University homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • Near the tub or toilet, sheet vinyl that is lifting, curling or turning gray
  • The room keeps a damp, musty smell even right after a thorough cleaning
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
How We Fix It

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Find the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Open What Is Needed

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Cavities

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. We apply antimicrobial treatment and confirm dry readings before anything is closed up.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few days

Water Slips Through

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Trapped Steam

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Damage Downstairs

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Mold Behind Tile

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.
