Restoration Challenges Cape Coral Homeowners Face

Water trouble is not all equally urgent, and knowing the difference helps you decide what to do first. The five below are ones we see constantly in Cape Coral, listed from most pressing to least.

Bursts: Nothing is more time-critical than a broken water line, which keeps pouring until the supply is cut. Cold weather, depending on the season, is one trigger, with the leak appearing as the frozen section thaws. Shut the house supply, run a tap to drain the lines and phone us immediately, whatever the hour. Ceilings that bulge with trapped water should be left alone and the room below kept empty.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. You cannot stop the rain, but you can move belongings up and keep away from any outlets near the water. Having passed over pavement and soil on the way in, it carries dirt and germs. As soon as it is safe to enter, we pump the water out and take away porous items it ruined.

Kitchens: Leaks from a dishwasher, an ice maker line or the plumbing under the kitchen sink should be dealt with the day you find them. Close the small valve under the sink or behind the appliance and the flow usually stops. The harm continues, though, since water has already run under cabinets and flooring where air does not move. In Cape Coral kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Moisture under a raised floor is seldom an emergency, but it should not be put off for a season. Puddles on the ground cover, dripping ductwork or wet insulation between the joists are what an inspection usually turns up. Given time, that humidity works its way upstairs and the joists start to rot. Our crew clears any standing water, brings the framing down to a normal moisture level and reports the likely cause.

Odors: Last on the list is a stale, earthy odor in a room that once had a leak, which is worth a scheduled visit. Since growth can start 24 to 48 hours after materials get wet, an old leak that was never fully dried is a likely culprit. Avoid wiping or bleaching a big area on your own, as that sends spores into other rooms. Found Water in Your Cape Coral Home? Start Here

What you do before help reaches your Cape Coral address has a real effect on how the job goes. Look after people first and property second. If water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel, stay out of it, and switch off power to that area only if you can reach the breaker without stepping in water. If the water is from a sewer or toilet backup, do not touch it at all.

Next, stop the source. Close the valve at the fixture if there is one, or the main shutoff for the house. After that, carry light items such as rugs, documents, electronics and chairs somewhere dry. Slide plastic or aluminum foil beneath the feet of anything too heavy to move, which stops stains and swelling.

Take photos and video of every affected room before you clean up, because your insurer will want to see the damage as it was. If the water is clean and shallow, blot what you can, and let our Cape Coral crew handle everything else.

Simple Ways to Keep a Cape Coral Home Dry

Water damage looks sudden, but the causes are usually slow: a hose that aged, a seal that dried out, a drain that clogged a little more each month. For homeowners in Cape Coral, prevention is mainly a matter of knowing where to look.

Pipes and fittings top the list. A water bill that climbs for no reason, or pressure that seems too high, can signal a concealed leak or strain on the system. Appliances are second. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. The roof and the ground outside are third. Missing shingles, cracked flashing, blocked gutters and soil that slopes toward the house all let rain find a way in.

Leaving the house empty for a while? Turn the water off at the main, or ask someone to check the house. University Restoration Co is glad to talk through prevention with any homeowner in Cape Coral. And if the worst happens anyway, a fast call keeps a small leak from turning into a large repair.
