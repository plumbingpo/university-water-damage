Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What University Homeowners Should Know

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. By the time the stain appears, water has already run along a joist, soaked the insulation and pooled on the back of the drywall. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. We trace it to the source before anything gets patched.

A coat of paint over the spot solves nothing. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. Sagging changes the picture. Keep clear of that area and phone us.

Signs to Look For

  • Drywall that sags, bows or feels soft when pressed gently
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
  • Hairline cracks spreading outward from a stained or damp spot
  • Drywall tape lifting and seams or screw heads starting to show
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

Water follows joists, pipes and ducts, so it can drip several feet from where it entered. Cutting in just under the mark may leave damp areas elsewhere untouched.

Electricity Overhead

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. Turn off that room's breaker, because a wet fixture may short out.

What the Upstairs Is Hiding

A wet ceiling usually means a wet subfloor or attic deck directly over it. Leaving it out of the drying plan lets dampness linger, so both levels get inspected.

Sudden Collapse

Once saturated, drywall weighs much more and no longer holds its screws well. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

What Can Be Saved

Dry It or Replace It?

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Trace the Leak

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Secure the Room

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. If the ceiling is holding water, we release it in a controlled way and catch it.

3

Remove and Dry Out

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

Once moisture readings are normal we install new insulation and drywall, tape and finish the seams, match the existing texture and prime with a stain blocker before painting.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

24 to 48 hours

Stains and Bubbles

You see the first ring, along with blisters in the paint. Mold can begin on the drywall paper and the wet insulation presses down on the panel.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

Past one week

Sections May Fall

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.
