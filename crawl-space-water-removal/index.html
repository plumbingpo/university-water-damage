Crawl Space Water Removal: What University Homeowners Should Know

Water under the house is easy to miss. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

Signs to Look For

  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Sweating pipes and ductwork that drip onto the ground beneath the home
  • A look through the hatch shows pooled water or soupy soil
  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Settling Under the House

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

What You Breathe Upstairs

A good share of the air on your first floor rises from the crawl space, so mold spores and damp odors down there end up in the rooms you live in.

Contaminated Water

Water under a house is often not clean. Groundwater full of soil and waste, or a drain leak, counts as Category 2 or 3, which calls for protective equipment and disinfecting.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First day

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Inspect and Make Safe

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Remove Standing Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

We bag up the wet insulation, damaged vapor barrier and loose debris and take it all outside. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Final Drying and Checks

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.
