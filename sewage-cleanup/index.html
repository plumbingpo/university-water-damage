Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. University Restoration Co provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout University, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
