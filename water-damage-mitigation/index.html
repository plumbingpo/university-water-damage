Mitigation means one thing: keeping the damage from getting any bigger. Repair is a different job. Mitigation covers the steps taken ahead of repairs so undamaged materials stay that way. In University, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. Each is its own phase of the job. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Insurance companies also bill the two as separate line items, which makes it useful to know your current phase.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. Source control, so no new water enters. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Containment, using plastic barriers and controlled airflow so moisture and contaminants stay out of clean rooms. Stabilization, which means removing materials that cannot be saved, treating surfaces with antimicrobial, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers. Along the way, your belongings and furniture get moved or set up on blocks to keep them safe.

Why Water Damage Mitigation Is Not a DIY Job

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Is the water Category 1, 2 or 3, and has it changed since the leak began? Does the cabinet toe kick need to come off? Our team makes these calls from training and from moisture readings, not from how a surface looks or feels to the hand.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Most policies require the owner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and insurers want proof it was done. On arrival we take photos, then log moisture readings every day and itemize whatever is removed. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. The repair contractor gets a clear place to start as well, without hidden moisture waiting behind fresh drywall.

What to Expect From Our Water Damage Mitigation

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose University Restoration Co:

1

Control the Source

The supply gets shut off, the broken line capped, or the opening letting water in tarped and boarded. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Extract the Water

We extract standing water using pumps and extraction units, then check carpet and its padding. Getting bulk water out quickly does more to limit damage than any amount of drying equipment alone.

Plastic barriers and controlled airflow separate wet rooms from dry ones. This keeps humidity, dust and any contaminants from travelling through the house while work is under way.

Signs You Need Water Damage Mitigation in University

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • After a leak, the baseboards have swollen or started separating from the wall.
  • A leak has been stopped but floors, walls or cabinets are still wet.
  • Plumbing repairs are done, but no one has addressed what got wet.
  • Carpet squishes underfoot, or the pad beneath it is saturated.
  • Water is moving from one room into the next along the flooring.
  • Bathroom or kitchen cabinets are sitting over water that cannot be reached to dry.
  • Rain is getting inside through a damaged roof or an open window gap.

Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
  • Braided steel supply lines should take the place of rubber ones on the washing machine about every five years.
  • Never switch the furnace fully off in freezing weather, since pipes can freeze and split.
  • Schedule an annual water heater inspection and swap the unit out before its tank gives way.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
